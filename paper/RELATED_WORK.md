# Related Work

## Attack Graphs and Security Metrics

Automated reasoning about multi-step compromise predates this proposal by decades. Sheyner, Haines, Jha, Lippmann and Wing's *Automated Generation and Analysis of Attack Graphs* used symbolic model checking to construct attack graphs and analyze defensive choices [S02]. Its relevant insight is that security depends on sequences of enabled actions, not isolated vulnerability counts. Blast Radius does not claim to invent reachability, attack graphs, privilege paths or automated counterfactual analysis. It narrows the object of measurement to the authorization consequences of a declared starting credential, over a finite resource-action inventory. The contribution under investigation is a shared measurement contract, including denominator rules, constraint assumptions, exact results and executable cross-implementation checks.

Ou, Govindavajhala and Appel's MulVAL integrates vulnerability descriptions, configurations and operating-system permission facts using Datalog [M05]. This is a stronger precedent than a generic graph analogy: it shows how heterogeneous evidence can feed a formal reasoning system while retaining explicit inference rules. Our core intentionally excludes arbitrary software exploits and limits transitions to declared authentication, membership, grants, assumptions and guarded credential acquisition. A MulVAL-style vulnerability extension could enlarge that model, but it would require a distinct profile. Silently representing a zero-day as an ordinary role assumption would conceal a change in the attacker's capabilities and invalidate comparisons.

Noel, Wang, Singhal and Jajodia model combinations of vulnerabilities, propagate attack likelihoods and compare mitigation costs in *Measuring Security Risk of Networks Using Attack Graphs* [N10]. Their work demonstrates that graph-based security measurement and prioritization are established research areas. Blast Radius deliberately does not attach an exploitation probability to a reachable permission or interpret a normalized fraction as expected loss. Its canonical quantity is a set cardinality ratio; weighted variants express declared sensitivity or action importance. The proof obligations are correspondingly narrower: bounds, fixed-universe monotonicity, finite termination and independent-source union behavior. These properties do not establish predictive validity, and concentration statistics such as Gini need separate interpretation because they are not monotone under permission removal.

## Authorization Semantics and Cloud Analysis

Backes and colleagues' Zelkova formalizes the AWS policy language and encodes policy properties in SMT [Z18]. It compares policy behaviors and detects policy misconfigurations using semantic reasoning. This addresses a difficult issue that a platform-neutral graph cannot solve merely by naming an edge: whether a permission is actually effective under the provider's policy language. A correct radius calculation on an incorrect normalized graph remains incorrect about the platform. We therefore separate core metric conformance from normalizer conformance and decline full AWS equivalence when conditions, service-control policies, permission boundaries or session policies are unsupported. Exact reference output is evidence about the declared graph, not certification of a cloud account's effective authorization.

AWS IAM Access Analyzer now documents external, internal and unused-access analysis, policy validation, custom policy checks and activity-based policy generation [A26]. It would be inaccurate to characterize existing IAM analysis as only checking whether a bucket is public. These capabilities overlap substantially with the inputs and operational questions motivating credential-reach measurement. The proposed standard is complementary: a provider-specific analyzer could supply justified normalized capabilities, while a separately implemented metric produces comparable values only within matching inventories and profiles. Observed inactivity is also different from potential reach; a credential unused during a logging window may retain extensive authority. Our current offline adapters do not establish integration with or parity to these services.

Cloud misconfiguration scanners likewise have a broader scope than a simple pass/fail checklist. Prowler's public repository describes security checks, weighted prioritization and an attack-path capability combining inventory with findings [P26]. BloodHound documents identity-relationship and attack-path analysis, including extensibility beyond its original directory-focused setting [B26]. These are substantive predecessors and potential implementers, not products to dismiss to manufacture novelty. Their current documentation also warns against using a stale comparison table: capabilities change. This project does not compare detection rates or claim superiority without a common independently labeled workload. A public suite can help expose disagreements in a specific metric without adjudicating every tool's collection coverage or broader security value.

## Secrets and Entitlement Management

Meli, McNiece and Reaves' *How Bad Can It Git?* studies secret leakage using longitudinal public-commit collection and a repository snapshot [L19]. It establishes that exposed authentication material is a recurring empirical phenomenon and carefully distinguishes its collection and detection methods. Secret discovery, however, does not itself determine the permissions attached to each discovered credential. Blast Radius starts from a declared compromised credential and measures modeled authorization reach; it neither scans public repositories for live keys nor validates exposed credentials against services. Incidents such as the Snowflake customer-account campaign motivate that starting condition, but retrospective disclosures rarely supply the complete preincident inventory needed for an actual normalized radius or percentile.

Cloud infrastructure entitlement management explicitly addresses effective permissions, inherited memberships, service-account relationships and role assumptions [C26]. A vendor-authored CIEM overview is useful evidence of the category's intended functionality, not an independent benchmark of coverage or an unbiased source for identity-population distributions. We do not import marketing ratios of machine to human identities into our synthetic generator. CIEM implementations could adopt the specification without surrendering their own interfaces or differentiation; no paid certification or exclusive connector access is necessary. The research question is whether an open measurement definition and shared fixtures reduce ambiguity across implementations, not whether entitlement analysis should be reinvented as another management console.

## Aggregate Data and Privacy

The proposed dataset creates a second research obligation: summaries can disclose organizations even when identifiers are removed. Narayanan and Shmatikov demonstrate how sparse, high-dimensional records can be linked using auxiliary knowledge despite nominal anonymity [NS07]. Their setting is movie ratings, not cloud authorization, so its attack success rates cannot be transferred to tenant summaries. The methodological warning does transfer: removing names is insufficient, and rounding requires measurement rather than reassurance. Our synthetic experiment operationalizes only a limited test, exact structural distinctness and nearest-neighbor distance. Finding all500 exact summaries unique falsifies the claim that the selected synthetic payload is anonymous, but does not estimate a real organization's re-identification probability.

Dwork and Roth provide the formal differential-privacy framework for bounded sensitivity, composition and postprocessing [DR]. We use tenant addition/removal as the adjacency relation and one contribution per tenant to a fixed histogram, then specify exact integer noise and noisy support screening. The privacy argument depends on those bounds and independent randomness, not on an informal k-anonymity label. Public seeds are appropriate only for synthetic reproducibility; using them for private data would reveal the noise. A neutral host, consent process, durable deduplication and release accounting are essential operational prerequisites. These controls do not make the proposed dataset representative, and the coarse released histogram cannot recover the unique sensitive-surface unions required by the registered NHI hypothesis.

## Position of This Work

Together, these predecessors locate the proposal as a standards-and-artifacts experiment. The metric formalizes a deliberately bounded authorization question; conformance tests compare implementations; public-incident dossiers expose modeling gaps; and a prospective data protocol makes empirical and privacy limitations inspectable. No claim of the first credential graph, first cloud-risk score or only public security dataset is justified by this review. The missing empirical parameter sources, unperformed red-team arm and absent real tenant sample remain limitations to resolve before a research submission makes broader claims.
